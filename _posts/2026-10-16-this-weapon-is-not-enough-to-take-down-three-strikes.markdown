"Hey, Trigger, you dumbass. Tell me something. What color's the sky up there?"

Trigger was one of those protagonists that really made me feel like I was the one. Ace Combat games have always had that effect on me. Bandai Namco, the same company that produced masterpieces like Pac-Man, also creates one of my all-time favorites: the Ace Combat series.

Project Aces, the development group responsible for most of the Ace Combat games, recently released *Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve*. The gameplay looked insane. (I unfortunately am unable to play it; next-gen games are genuinely a pain to get running on my modest work laptop.)

But obviously, that did NOT mean I couldn't go back to *Skies Unknown*, the seventh game in the series and the predecessor to *Wings of Theve*. I also ended up rerunning *Ace Combat Zero: The Belkan War* AND *Project Wingman* (a different game altogether, independent of Ace Combat, which will definitely have a blog post of its own).

The reruns really awoke something in me. All these games I've been playing haven't given me nostalgia like Ace Combat did. Take, for example, *Project I.G.I.* - a game that I used to play with my dad when I was so little I couldn't figure out how to exit the game. Ace Combat has always broken the limits of what a game could make me feel. I personally CANNOT play a game for long if it doesn't make me feel happy, but *Ace Combat 7*? I've finished the whole game about 6 to 7 times. It is insane.

## Stick with Trigger

BUT WHY? What's so different about Ace Combat? It just makes you feel like... you're THE ONE. 

*"Stick with Trigger and you'll make it."*

That's what the NPC wingmen say. And you're Trigger. Imagine taking down around 5 bandits within the span of two minutes, and then pushing into an airspace with more than 50 enemies and TAKING THEM DOWN. With an unending amount of ammo, hyper-responsive physics, and a soaring orchestral score pounding in your ears, the power fantasy hits unlike anything else in modern gaming.

## The Legend of Ace Combat 7

Set in the year 2019 on the continent of Usea, Ace Combat 7: Skies Unknown follows the outbreak of the Lighthouse War between the Osean Federation and the Kingdom of Erusea. The conflict centers around the International Space Elevator (ISEV) - also known as "Lighthouse" - a massive structure built by Osea to power the continent's recovery, which Erusea views as Osean imperialist expansion.

The Fall and the Penal Unit: You play as Trigger, a top-tier Osean fighter pilot initially assigned to the Mage Squadron. During a high-stakes mission to rescue former Osean President Vincent Harling at the Space Elevator, Harling's aircraft is shot down. Trigger is falsely accused of his assassination, court-martialed, and exiled to the Spare Squadron - a sacrificial penal unit stationed at 444 Air Base.

Rising from "Dumbass" to Legend: Sent on near-suicidal sorties in scrap aircraft to act as radar bait and cannon fodder, Trigger repeatedly pulls off impossible tactical victories. His incredible flying earns the respect of his cynical squadmates and commander (leading to Count’s iconic quote: "Hey, Trigger, you dumbass. Tell me something. What color's the sky up there?").

The Tide Turns: Recognizing Trigger’s undeniable talent, the Osean military pardons him and reassigns him as the flight lead of the elite Strider Squadron under Long Caster's AWACS support.

The Main Threats: Throughout the campaign, Trigger faces two major obstacles:

1. Mihaly A. Shilage: A legendary, grandpa Erusean ace pilot whose combat data is being harvested to develop hyper-advanced AI drones.

2. Arsenal Ships & Drone Swarms: Massive airborne fortresses (Liberty and Justice) capable of launching hundreds of lethal, hyper-agile unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs).

Chaos and the Final Stand: As the war escalates, a satellite network crash plunges the continent into total communications blackout, causing rogue factions to form on both sides. Erusea's automated drone factories go rogue, launching autonomous production. Trigger eventually downs Mihaly, uniting surviving Osean and Erusean pilots under a single banner to destroy the remaining Arsenal Ship and defeat the advanced Hugin and Munin AI drones beneath the Space Elevator, restoring peace and securing the future of the skies.

## Wrapping up

Maybe because of my inability to play the recent slew of amazing releases, or maybe, just maybe, because Trigger made me feel like I was IT, Ace Combat will forever be the game I keep going back to. Ace Combat isn't just about flying planes - it's about how a game can make you feel entirely invincible for a few hours. That's all for this one. Until the next dogfight, keep your head up and stick with Trigger.
